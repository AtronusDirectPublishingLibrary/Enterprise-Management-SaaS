V
Executive Summary

The Ethio-BioPharma Enterprise (EBPE) Strategic Plan for the 2026-2030 period establishes
a comprehensive framework for achieving pharmaceutical and vaccine sovereignty of
Ethiopia. Established in 2025 as a state-owned strategic enterprise under the oversight of
Ethiopian Investment Holdings (EIH), EBPE serves as the primary vehicle for transforming
Ethiopia from an import-dependent nation into a regional biopharmaceutical manufacturing
hub- addressing the vulnerabilities exposed by the global COVID-19 pandemic.

The strategic architecture of EBPE is built upon an integrated campus model located in
Kilinto Industrial Park, designed to co-locate multiple synergistic business units including
human vaccine production (ShieldVax), generic medicines, diagnostics, Personal Protective
Equipment (PPE) and medical supplies manufacturing. This integrated model leverages
shared infrastructure, utilities, and workforce development systems to maximize
operational efficiency while enabling coordinated growth across the enterprise.

A core element of EBPE's financial architecture is the Quick-Win cross-subsidization model.
Under this model, the diagnostics, and medicines units will be operationalized first to
generate early revenue streams and internal liquidity. A portion of these profits will be
reinvested to support the development and scaling of the ShieldVax vaccine manufacturing
facility. By 2030, EBPE aims to commission an advanced vaccine fill-finish facility, achieve
operational profitability across all business units, and contribute to government goal of
localizing the manufaturing of essential medicines.

The financial investment for the five-year period centers on the development of a world-
class biopharmaceutical ecosystem, requiring a total capital investment exceeding USD
207 million by 2030. A critical cornerstone of this roadmap is the ShieldVax vaccine facility,
which serves the dual strategic objectives of achieving national self-reliance in vaccine
manufacturing and ensuring robust pandemic preparedness for future global health
emergencies.

The ShieldVax project requires a total investment of USD 150 million within this strategic
window to support the phased development of fill-finish operations, with commercial
production projected to commence in 2029. While the facility is a vital security asset, it is
also financially transformative; over a 30-year horizon, cumulative revenues are projected
to exceed USD 6.5 billion. With a calculated internal rate of return (IRR) of 11.8% and a
projected break-even point in approximately 15 years, ShieldVax represents a sustainable
intersection of public health sovereignty and long-term economic viability.

To mitigate the financing challenges typical of such long-term biopharmaceutical projects,
the Medicines and Diagnostics, PPE & Medical Supplies units will serve as EBPE's "Quick-
Win" early revenue generators. Designed to capture segments of Ethiopia's rapidly
expanding pharmaceutical market and advance national import-substitution goals, both
units demonstrate strong commercial viability. Financial projections indicate highly
attractive returns: the rapid diagnostic test (RDT) segment boasts an IRR of 39.26% with a

VI
rapid payback period of 4.33 years, while the Medicines unit achieves operational
profitability and positive free cash flow by its third year of operation. Through a deliberate
cross-subsidization strategy, these units will generate the early operating cash flows and
internal liquidity required to sustain the capital-intensive development of the broader
enterprise.
To date, the enterprise has secured or identified USD 81.0 million in high-potential financing,
including USD 7.0 million in equity from Ethiopian Investment Holdings (EIH) and an
anticipated USD 74.0 million from the World Bank's HEPRR program through Ministry of
Health (MoH). However, this funding is allocated exclusively to the ShieldVax facility.
Consequently, the enterprise faces a consolidated funding gap of USD 126.04 million,
comprising USD 69.0 million to sustain ShieldVax, USD 49.8 million for the Medicines unit,
and USD 7.24 million for Diagnostics and Medical Supplies. To bridge this gap, EBPE will
deploy a multi-tiered, blended finance strategy. This includes structured debt-to-equity
financing for the Quick-Win units (targeting 30% and 40% equity for Medicines and
Diagnostics, respectively), concessional loans from development finance institutions (DFI),
and strategic joint ventures (JV) with global pharmaceutical firms. This approach will allow
the enterprise to mobilize capital while maintaining financial sustainability and ensuring
that investments are aligned with the phased development of the integrated campus.
Alignment with continental goals is a cornerstone of this strategic plan. It is systematically
mapped to the African Union's Agenda 2063, The Lusaka Agenda, and the Africa CDC's
Partnerships for African Vaccine Manufacturing (PAVM) roadmap which targets the local
production of 60% of Africa's vaccines and pharmaceuticals by 2040. Furthermore, the
enterprise leverages Ethiopia's unique competitive advantages, including its status as a
logistical epicenter supported by Ethiopian Airlines, its vast domestic market of over 130
million population and ever-expanding health insurance scheme, to de-risk high-tech
manufacturing investments. The 2026-2030 Strategic Plan serves as a definitive roadmap
for achieving health security, economic resilience, and industrial excellence, ensuring that
Ethiopia emerges as a pivotal actor in the African biopharmaceutical landscape.

VII
1. Introduction

Ethiopia's pharmaceutical sector is strategically important but remains hihgly dependent on
imports, which supply over 80% of the market. This vulnerability was starkly exposed during
the COVID-19 pandemic, when global supply chain disruptions severely constrained access
to essential medicines and vaccines. In response, the Government of Ethiopia prioritized
local pharmaceutical manufacturing, thorugh national spolicy frameworks and trategies
including the National Health Policy, Manufacturing Industry Policy, The Home Grown
Economic Reform, Health Sector Medium Term Development and Investment Plan (HSDIP),
the National Strategy and Plan of Action for Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Development
(NSPA-Pharma) 2015-2025, PAVM and the African Union's Lusaka Agenda, which taims to
increase local pharmaceuticals production across Africa to 60% by 2040.

Ethiopia's local pharmaceuticals production remained low due to dependency for
importation of more than 90% of active pharmaceutical ingredinets (API) and excipients, low
capacity utilization, constrained by foreign exchange shortages, and limited technological
capability. Notably, no local manufacturer holds World Health Organization Prequalification
(WHO-PQ) for pharmaceuticals and vaccines, resulting in the continued dependence on
imported products for most donor-funded procurement.

Market assessments further highlight the structural imbalance between domestic
production and national demand. In EFY 2016, local production accounted for 13.9% of
medicines, 17.5% of medical supplies, and only 1.9% of medical equipment. Chemicals and
laboratory reagents represented an exception, with 63.4% local production, indicating that
targeted investment and enabling policy frameworks can significantly improve domestic
production capacity.

The pharmaceutical market, including vaccines, is also heavily donor-dependent, with more
than 50% of national procurement and approximately 85% of Ethiopian Pharmaceuticals
Supply Service (EPSS) purchases financed externally. Vaccine supply is almost entirely
import-based, exposing Ethiopia to external shocks and geopolitical supply constraints.

To address these systemic challenges, the Government established Ethio-BioPharma Group
in 2023, along with its subsidiary ShieldVax Enterprise following a Council of Ministers
Regulation, to build national capacity for local manufacturing of pharmaceuticals. The
immediate strategy focuses on operationaliziing ShieldVax and developing human vaccine
fill-and-finish capabilities as a foundation for eventual full-scale product development and
manufacturing. As part of institutional restructuring to enhance sustainability and
commercial viability, the current reform process proposes dissolving ShieldVax as a
separate legal entity and integrating it as a business unit under EBPE, thereby streamlining

1
governance and strengthening operational efficiency while preserving public service
objectives.

The establishment of EBPE under EIH represents a shift in Ethiopia's approach to developing
strategic manufacturing industry capabilities. By positioning the enterprise under EIH, the
Government oe Ethiopia (GoE) signals a shift toward professionally governed, commercially
oriented state participation in high-technology manufacturing. This arrangement provides
EBPE with structured oversight, institutional credibility, and the governance architecture
necessary to manage complex, large-scale transformation initiatives.

This 2026 - 2030 Strategic Plan is designed to operationalize the enterprise's mandate
through a phased build-out of infrastructure, workforce, and regulatory systems. It moves
beyond the conceptual phase into the active construction and commissioning of an
integrated biopharmaceutical campus. It sets the stage for a narrative of industrial
transformation, where Ethiopia utilizes its logistical dominance, diplomatic influence, and
demographic scale to secure its health future.

2. Strategic Context & Rationale

The strategic rationale for EBPE is rooted in resolving systemic dependencies that threaten
national security, economic stability, and long-term health resilience. Across Africa,
approximately 99% of vaccines and more than 70% of medicines are imported, creating
structural exposure to external supply disruptions. For Ethiopia which imports more than
80% of pharmaceuticals, this dependency translates into supply chain vulnerability,
sustained pressure on foreign exchange reserves, and limited pandemic preparedness.

Positioning EBPE under EIH reflects a deliberate strategic decision to anchor
pharmaceutical industrialization within a strong sovereign investment framework capable of
mobilizing capital and enforcing financial discipline. As Ethiopia's strategic investment arm,
EIH provides governance discipline, financial oversight, and access to long-term capital
required for large-scale, capital-intensive biopharmaceutical projects. This structure
enables EBPE to operate as a commercially disciplined enterprise while advancing national
health security objectives, effectively bridging public policy ambition with industrial
execution and transforming pharmaceutical sovereignty into a viable and investable national
agenda.

EBPE serves as the operational vehicle for implementing Ethiopia's pharmaceutical
manufacturing agenda. It translates the government ambitions under the national health
policy, manufacturing industry policy and the home-grown economic reform and related
frameworks into tangible investments in infrastructure, innovation, technology transfer, and
workforce development, with the overarching objective of ensuring national health security.

2
The enterprise is also strategically positioned within a shifting global health architecture.
International partners such as Global Alliance for Vaccine and Immunization (Gavi), the
Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI), Africa CDC and the World Bank are
increasingly supporting regional manufacturing hubs to decentralize global supply chains
and strengthen regional self-reliance. This creates a timely opportunity for Ethiopia to
position itself as a credible destination for biopharmaceutical investment and strategic
partnerships.

Ethiopia's alrge domestic demand provides an anchor market that mitigates the commercial
risks associated with vaccine and biopharmaceutical production. With a population
exceeding 130 million and a triple burden of communicable and rising non-communicable
diseases and injuries, there is a huge sustained long-term demand for pharmaceuticals. The
cargo and logistics capabilities of Ethiopian Airlines provide a competitive advantage for
regional distribution within Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA) and
the East African Community (EAC), positioning Ethiopia as a potential pharmaceutical
gateway for Eastern and Southern Africa. Additionally, EBPE's mission is underpinned by the
African Union's Agenda 2063, Lusaka Agenda and the PAVM roadmap, which targets the
local production of 60% of Africa's vaccine and other health products need by 2040.

Together, these factors establish a compelling case for EBPE as both a national security
instrument and a commercially viable industrial platform capable of advancing
pharmaceutical sovereignty, regional integration, and sustainable economic
transformation. By establishing a fully integrated biopharmaceutical campus, EBPE aims to
transform Ethiopia from a passive recipient of global health aid into a self-reliant producer
and a leading exporter within the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA).

3. Institutional Mandate & Business Model

EBPE's institutional mandate is to achieve pharmaceutical and vaccine sovereignty through
a self-sustaining ecosystem of manufacturing businesses. The enterprise operates through
an integrated corporate, managing a portfolio of specialized units on a unified campus.

3.1. Business Units and Synergy

The business units are designed to be synergistic, sharing infrastructure, utilities, and

corporate functions to achieve maximum efficiency.

· ShieldVax: The flagship unit dedicated to end-to-end human vaccine manufacturing. It
    will first focus on fill-and-finish operations for essential vaccines before moving into drug
    substance production.

· Medicine and Input Material Unit: A unit focused on manufacturing essential generic
    medicines (tablets, capsules, and injectables,etc). This unit incorporates the future

3
    production of Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs) and excipients to reduce import
    dependency for its own production lines.

· Diagnostics, PPE, and Medical Supplies Unit: A unit designed for rapid revenue generation.
    It focuses on the production of rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs), molecular diagnostics, PPE,
    medical supplies and surgical kits.

3.2. The Integrated Campus Model

The integrated campus model is a critical operational innovation. By co-locating all units,
EBPE achieves economies of scale in shared utilities, shared infrastructure (utilities, waste
management, R&D labs), centralized corporate level procurement. This proximity facilitates
a unified talent development program where staff can be cross trained across different
manufacturing disciplines.

3.3. The Financial Cross-Subsidization Strategy

To overcome the financing challenges typical of biopharmaceutical projects in emerging
markets, EBPE employs a tiered launch strategy. The "Quick-Win" units (PPE, Diagnostics,
and medicines) are engineered for rapid commissioning and early revenue generation. A
formal mandate requires the reinvestment of its profits into the capital and operational
expenses of the ShieldVax vaccine facility. This ensures that the flagship project is
supported by a stable internal revenue stream, reducing the risk of project stalling due to
external funding fluctuations.

4. Situational Analysis of the Pharmaceutical Sector

Ethiopia's pharmaceutical sector is strategically vital for strengthening national health
security, mitigating reliance on imports, and reducing foreign exchange pressures. Despite
this strategic importance, the country remains heavily dependent on imports for both
finished pharmaceutical products and essential production inputs.

4.1. Market Overview and Import Dependency

The Ethiopian pharmaceutical and medical device market is on a rapid expansion trajectory.
Valued at approximately USD 1 billion in 2021, the market grew to an estimated USD 1.8
billion by 2025, with projections indicating it could exceed USD 4 billion by 2030, driven by
population growth and expanded health coverage. Despite this robust demand, local
production has historically captured only a limited share of the market. In 2021, local
manufacturers supplied approximately USD 59.1 million worth of medicines and supplies,
representing just 6.1% of the total market value. By 2022, approximately 4% of total
purchased products were locally manufactured, a level that remains significantly lower that

4
African peers and marks a sharp decline from previous years when local production
accounted for nearly a quarter of the market share.

Public procurement, managed primarily by the EPSS, constitutes a major component of the
market. EPSS data indicate a decline in local sourcing for general pharmaceuticals, which
fell from 24% in 2010 EFY to just 8% in 2014 EFY. This performance remains significantly
below the national target to source 47% of pharmaceuticals from local manufacturers by
2017 EFY. However, specific sub-sector analysis reveals areas where targeted policy
interventions have improved local participation. EPSS's trend analysis covering the period
2012-2017 EFY notes that in targeted categories where EPSS manages the procurement of
the commodities, the local manufacturing share of total procurement has risen to 41%
(show the below figure), driven by specific policy interventions.

Assessment data reveals notable discrepancies across product categories. In the
medicines sector, local production accounted for 9% of total consumption in 2014 EFY,
rising marginally to 13.9% by 2016 EFY. Medical supplies saw a moderate increase from 13%
to 17.5% over the same period. Conversely, the chemicals and laboratory reagents category
demonstrated significant import substitution potential, growing from 17% to 63.4% by 2016
EFY. The sector remains heavily characterized by donor dependence, which funds over 50%
of all pharmaceutical procurement and 85% of EPSS purchases. Consequently, essential
program medicines such as antiretrovirals (ARVs), antimalarials, and anti-tuberculosis
drugs remain nearly 100% imported, primarily from India and China.

4.2. Production Capacity and Portfolio

As of 2024, Ethiopia has approximately 13 local manufacturers of pharmaceuticals,
comprising a mix of local ownership, JV, and foreign direct investment. A critical operational

5
bottleneck is the sector's low capacity utilization, which averages between 14% and 18%,
far below the 60% threshold required for operational sustainability. This underutilization has
contributed to a decline in production volumes where tablet production fell by 46% and
capsule production by 69% between 2018/19 and 2022/23. However, some improvement
has been observed in recent years.

The product portfolio of local firms is currently limited to basic formulations, including
common antibiotics, analgesics, and selected chronic disease medicines. While local
production meets approximately 50% of the consumption demand for antibiotics, there is a
complete absence of local manufacturing for other essential medicines like ARVs and anti-
TB drugs. This gap represents a missed opportunity in a USD 100 million import market.
Furthermore, no local manufacturer held WHO-PQ certification, and only six possessed
Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) certification, effectively excluding local firms from the
large donor-funded market segment.

4.3 Policy and Enabling Environment

The government has instituted a range of policy incentives to stimulate the sector. Economic
reforms include the establishment of Kilinto Industrial Park dedicated for pharmaceutical
manufacturing. Fiscal incentives include corporate income tax holidays of up to six years,
customs duty exemptions on capital goods and eligible inputs, and full export duty
exemptions. In the procurement domain, EPSS has revised its pharmaceutical procurement
specific directives which gives preference to domestically manufactured pharmaceuticals
and up to a 25% price preference for local manufacturers in international open tenders and
up to a 30% advance payment for local manufacturers. Additionally, the introduction of
long-term framework agreements spanning up to seven years aims to provide market
stability.

Regulatory advancements have also been achieved. The Ethiopian Food and Drug Authority
(EFDA) successfully attained WHO Maturity Level 3 (ML3) in September 2025, a milestone
that signifies a stable and well-functioning regulatory system. This achievement is a critical
prerequisite for enabling local manufacturers to access international and donor-funded
markets and eventually achieve WHO-PQ.

4.4 Key Challenges

Despite the supportive policy framework, the sector faces a multitude of challenges. Access
to working capital and foreign exchange is universally identified as the most critical barrier
by local manufacturers. Manufacturers face delays averaging 2 - 3 months to access FX,
compounded by high transaction costs and interest rates ranging from 15% to 22% limiting
the investment by the private sector.

6
High import dependency extends to APIs and packaging materials, exacerbated by the lack
of local pooled procurement mechanisms among manufacturers. Technical and human
capital gaps also persist, characterized by a shortage of skilled personnel for equipment
calibration and maintenance, forcing reliance on foreign experts. Finally, procurement
inflexibilities, such as EPSS spot tenders lacking price variation adjustment mechanisms
and limited margin for framework agreement force manufacturers to absorb severe FX and
inflation shocks.

6. The Five Strategic Pillars

The implementation of the EBPE strategy is supported by five strategic pillars that address
infrastructure, technology, regulation, people, and markets.

Pillar 1: Infrastructure and Integrated Biopharmaceutical Campus
Development

The primary focus is the establishment of an integrated biopharmaceutical campus in
Kilinto Industrial Park. This integrated campus will serve as the physical heart of EBPE,
balancing quick-win units with the long-term infrastructure for vaccine production,
expansion of the quick-wins, and maximizing shared utilization of utilities.

Pillar 2: Technology Transfer, Smart Manufacturing, R&D and Innovation

EBPE will take the leadership in shifting the domestic pharmaceutical industry toward high-
tech manufacturing of vaccines, biologics and advanced medicines by following a
structured technology transfer process to absorb international knowledge. This pillar
focuses on forming technology transfer partnerships with international firms to start with
fill-finish and proceed to innovate beyond simple generics. Long-term intiatives include
developing local API capabilities and implementing digital systems supported by AI to
leverage latest developments in the sector.

The technology trasfer for vaccine, medicines and other health products manufacturing will
generally be done in three phases:

Phase 1: Packaging and Secondary Processing (Years 1-2)

During this initial phase, EBPE will establish early operational capability by focusing on
packaging and basic quality control (QC) activities using unlabelled, in-bulk vaccine vials
supplied by the technology transfer partner. Activities will include material receipt and
storage, primary and secondary packaging and labeling, and basic QC testing (such as
description and identity). This phase enables rapid entry into the vaccine value chain while
building foundational operational systems, regulatory processes, and workforce readiness.

Phase 2: Fill-and-Finish Operations (Years 2-4)

In this phase, EBPE will expand into fill-and-finish manufacturing using formulated, ready-
to-fill (RTF) bulk vaccines supplied by the partner. In addition to continuing activities from

11
Phase 1, EBPE will undertake pooling and mixing of bulk materials, aseptic filling and
sealing, final lot testing, stability testing, and expanded QC operations. This phase
represents a significant progression in technical capability and regulatory complexity,
allowing EBPE to increase value addition and strengthen its manufacturing capacity.

Phase 3: Drug Substance Handling and Formulation (Years 5-8)

In the final phase, EBPE will transition to handling purified bulk (drug substance) supplied by
the partner and will assume full responsibility for downstream manufacturing operations.
Activities will include compounding, formulation, drug substance QC and release, as well
as all activities from the previous phases. This phase positions EBPE as an advanced
vaccine manufacturer with integrated downstream capabilities, aligned with the long-term
objective of achieving end-to-end manufacturing readiness.

Pillar 3: Regulatory Excellence and Quality Assurance

EBPE will institutionalize regulatory excellence and quality assurance as the fundamental
backbone of its operational lifecycle, ensuring that regulatory approval is an integrated, non-
negotiable component of every project phase from inception to market. Central to this
strategy is the mandate that early and continuous engagement with the EFDA is critical.
EBPE will maintain a proactive partnership with the EFDA to ensure facility designs,
processes, and products align with national standards from day one. By leveraging the
EFDA's WHO ML3 status, the Enterprise will systematically guide its manufacturing units
toward achieving WHO-PQ, a vital milestone for entering donor-funded, regional, and
international markets.

Furthermore, EBPE will implement and maintain rigorous ISO certifications--specifically
focusing on ISO 9001 for quality management and ISO 13485 where applicable--to
standardize operational excellence and build global trust. This comprehensive framework
will not only secure local market authorization but also ensure full alignment with AMA for
regional harmonization, positioning EBPE as a compliant and competitive leader in the
global biopharmaceutical landscape.

Pillar 4: Human Capital Development and Talent Management

Over the next five years, EBPE will establish a dedicated Workforce Development Center
within its compound, institutionalizing specialized training in GMP, product development,
QA/QC, advanced biomanufacturing, regulatory science, and equipment calibration and
maintenance. This center will serve as the foundation for mitigating critical skill gaps while
fostering a culture of operational excellence. To ensure long-term sustainability, EBPE will
implement a comprehensive Leadership and Succession Planning Framework,

12
complemented by a Diaspora Talent Engagement Initiative designed to facilitate high-level
knowledge transfer.

Furthermore, the strategy prioritizes a robust talent pipeline through postgraduate
scholarships in pharmaceutical sciences and international technical partnerships that
provide staff with global exposure. Retention will be driven by a meritocratic environment
featuring performance-based incentives, clear career progression pathways, and a
steadfast commitment to gender inclusion and youth employment. By integrating digital
skills training and structured professional development, EBPE will build a resilient, high-
caliber workforce capable of maintaining a competitive edge in the global
biopharmaceutical landscape.

Pillar 5: Market Development and Strategic Partnership

Leveraging Ethiopia's domestic market of over 130 million people as anchor demand is
critical to de-risking investments. EBPE will utilize EPSS's domestic preference and long-
term framework agreements to ensure market stability and penetrate the private sector
market. The strategy also targets exports within the APPM, AfCFTA framework and
international market.

EBPE will embed Industry 4.0 technologies into its manufacturing facilities from the

predictive maintenance of equipment.

AI will be deployed for demand forecasting and supply chain optimization to mitigate
logistical delays. On the production side, AI-driven models will optimize yields and monitor

EBPE will establish a central data warehouse protected by a robust cybersecurity framework
and secure cloud infrastructure. This ensures data integrity which is critical for EFDA and

the global health security financing ecosystem. Leveraging sovereign backing, EBPE will
work to secure large-scale concessional loans, targeting AfDB, IFC and other DFIs. To offset
the capital intensity of the vaccine facility, EBPE will pursue development assistnace from
global health partners, including GAVI, CEPI and others. Strategic JV with leading global
vaccine manufacturing firms will also be negotiated, aiming to secure additional resource in
exchange for market access and technology transfer agreements.

3. Financial Discipline & Risk Hedging

Securing capital is only the first step; protecting it from macroeconomic volatility is critical
to the Strategic Plan's success. Once the Quick-Win units reach operational profitability by
a mandatory policy will be in place to reinvest their profits directly into ShieldVax operations.
Dedicated bridge financing will safeguard the ShieldVax launch against global market
volatility and potential delays in disbursements during the Phase 2 period. To mitigate
Ethiopia's structural foreign exchange constraints, EBPE will use hard-currency export
